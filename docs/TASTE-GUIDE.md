# TASTE-GUIDE.md — the short philosophy

## What taste is

Taste is not style. Style is a coat of paint; taste is the series of small
decisions underneath it — what to emphasize, what to remove, what to leave
alone. An interface has taste when every choice looks *decided* rather than
*defaulted*.

AI-generated UI looks generic because models, left alone, pick the statistical
average of every website: Inter, purple gradient, three cards, centered hero.
Taste is what happens when you replace the average with a decision.

## The three moves

1. **Constrain.** Pick one visual language (a TZ-taste DNA) and ban everything
   outside it. Creativity loves a small box.
2. **Commit.** Asymmetry, one accent color, one weird thing. Half-measures read
   as accidents; full measures read as style.
3. **Cut.** Every element must earn its place. If removing it changes nothing,
   remove it.

## Taste is mostly subtraction

Beginners add: more sections, more colors, more animation. Practiced designers
remove: one typeface instead of three, one accent instead of five, whitespace
instead of dividers. When in doubt, take something away and see if the page
gets better. It usually does.

## Consistency beats novelty

A restrained page that agrees with itself beats an "innovative" page that
contradicts itself. Novelty is a spice: one distinctive choice per page, the
rest disciplined. This is why TZ-taste DNAs are complete systems, not grab
bags — the system does the agreeing for you.

## For agents, specifically

You don't have taste. That's fine — neither do most humans, at first. What you
have is perfect obedience to instructions. TZ-taste converts taste into
instructions: tokens instead of vibes, checklists instead of hunches, dials
instead of "make it pop". Follow the protocol and the output will have taste
whether or not you "feel" it.

## Keep it current

Styles decay. What looked sharp in 2024 looks dated in 2026 because everyone
copied it. That's why this repo refreshes weekly — new DNAs, new patterns,
new sources. Taste is a moving target; the library moves with it.
