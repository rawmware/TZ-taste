# Vinyl Crate

> Saturday in the record store: sleeve grids in tight rows, kraft dividers between genres, sticker prices on everything. Analog warmth with a crate-digger's filing discipline.

## Type

- **Display:** Alfa Slab One — the painted storefront-sign voice: massive, unapologetic, all-caps. Section titles and the shop name only; it's a shout, not a whisper.
- **Body:** DM Sans — the counter voice: new arrivals, staff picks, descriptions. Warm and readable, never shouting over the signage.
- **Mono:** Space Mono — catalog numbers, prices, conditions, genre codes. Sticker text and spine labels live here ("NM · $18 · SOUL").

Google Fonts: `Alfa+Slab+One`, `DM+Sans:opsz,wght@9..40,400;9..40,500;9..40,700`, `Space+Mono:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Grid the sleeves tight: uniform squares in rows with kraft dividers labeling each row's genre in mono.
- Stickers and labels tilt slightly — real price stickers are never straight.
- Corners square for sleeves, small radius for stickers; the crate is rectangular and honest.
- Generous gaps between crates (sections), tight gaps within — the store has aisles and shelves.

## Motion

Browsing motion: sleeves lift and tilt slightly on hover like you're flipping past them in the bin; a tapped sleeve slides out and back. Section transitions are quick horizontal wipes, like walking to the next bin. Nothing fades in slowly — the store is already open.

## Do

- Show things as square sleeves with a spine shadow — the square format is the whole visual system.
- Label every row with a kraft divider: "SOUL / FUNK", "STAFF PICKS", "NEW ARRIVALS" in mono.
- Sticker-price everything: a rotated mono sticker on each item with price and condition.
- Keep one "now spinning" panel — a featured item with a tonearm-style detail or a slowly rotating record.

## Don't

- Don't design album-cover art here — this DNA is the store that sells the sleeves, not the sleeves themselves.
- Don't use the display font for anything small — Alfa Slab One below 24px is just a heavy blob.
- Don't go digital-dark — the warm paper and kraft are the differentiator against streaming-service aesthetics.
- Don't round the sleeves — vinyl is square, and so is this.

## The one weird thing

Add a "flip" interaction: hovering a sleeve row makes the sleeves ripple through with a slight scale wave, left to right, like fingers flipping through a bin. Or put a "now spinning" footer bar: a thin strip showing the current featured record with a tiny record that rotates via CSS animation and mono track info beside it. Pick one.

## Best for

Record stores, music blogs, college radio, hi-fi shops, label catalogs.

## Lineage

The filing logic of record bins — genre dividers, condition codes, sticker pricing — as information design; the painted-sign tradition of storefront typography; the principle that in a great shop, everything is labeled and nothing is precious.
