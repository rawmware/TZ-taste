# Matchbook Cover

> Two inches of advertising you kept in your pocket.
> Strike anywhere, remember everything.

## Type

- **Display:** Limelight (deco matchbook lettering — the cover art headline, 3–5rem)
- **Body:** Josefin Sans (1rem, the small print under the art)
- **Mono:** Inconsolata (phone numbers, cautions — "CLOSE COVER BEFORE STRIKING")

Google Fonts: `Limelight` `Josefin+Sans:wght@400;500;700` `Inconsolata:wght@400;600`

## Spacing & shape

- Content is laid out as a row of matchbook covers: small, dense, collectible panels.
- A striker strip — a textured repeating band in browns — divides major sections.
- Covers get generous space between them; each one is a tiny poster.
- Radius: `3px`. Card stock, barely eased.

## Motion

Covers fan a few degrees on hover (rotate, transform only). The matchstick CTA
"strikes" on hover — a quick flare of the accent (opacity/scale, transform and
opacity only). With reduced motion: the matches stay in the box.

## Do

- Write old-school ad copy: "Open late. Park out back. Tell them the matchbook sent you."
- Use tiny line-art motifs — a martini glass, a key, a phone — drawn, not clip-art.
- Run the striker strip as the footer motif; it should feel good to scroll past.
- Keep every cover's message to one line. Two inches is not a lot of room.

## Don't

- No purple or blue gradients — matchbooks are printed flat, in few colors.
- No fire emoji; the matchstick is drawn or it's not there.
- No fake vintage brands presented as real history.
- No lorem — a matchbook never shipped with placeholder copy.

## The one weird thing

The primary CTA is a matchstick: on hover it strikes — a quick flare of the
accent — and the label changes to "Struck. See you Friday."
