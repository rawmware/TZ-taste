# Farmers Almanac

> Print reference craft: aged paper, inked tables, the moon doing the scheduling.
> Weather wisdom set by hand, one season at a time.

## Type

- **Display:** Pirata One (mastheads and drop caps only — a little blackletter goes a long way)
- **Body:** Spectral (1.05rem, almanac prose with long measures and room to breathe)
- **Mono:** Courier Prime (planting tables, moon times — "Full moon 21:44")

Google Fonts: `Pirata+One` `Spectral:wght@400;500;700` `Courier+Prime:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Two-column almanac pages with ruled columns; entries separated by hairlines.
- Generous page margins, like the white edge of a printed page.
- Tables banded in the darker paper tone, dense and honest.
- Radius: `0px`. The press has no corners.

## Motion

Almost none — print does not move. The moon-phase marker fades in once
(opacity only) and then sits still for the season.
With reduced motion: fully still.

## Do

- Write like the almanac: "Plant peas when the forsythia blooms, not before."
- Set real data tables in mono — frost dates, moon phases, tide notes.
- Spend the harvest red once per page: the moon table or the CTA.
- Microcopy from the farmhouse: "Last frost: May 12, give or take a week."

## Don't

- No purple or blue gradients — almanac paper is flat and aged, never a gradient sky.
- No farm kitsch: no cartoon barns, no clip-art chickens, nothing cute.
- No emoji icons; no lorem — the almanac is full of real information.
- No sleek dark mode. This is paper; keep it paper.

## The one weird thing

A moon-phase marker pinned in the header — "Waxing gibbous" with a small
drawn moon — set differently per page, in mono. The planting advice on the
page quietly follows it: above-ground crops for waxing, roots for waning.
