# Postcard Rack

> Tourist ephemera: sun-faded ink, a spinning rack of somewhere-elses,
> and handwriting on the back that says the weather is perfect.

## Tokens

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `#f6efdd` | Sun-faded card stock |
| `--surface` | `#efe3c8` | Darker card — rack dividers |
| `--ink` | `#3d2f22` | Postcard ink |
| `--muted` | `#8a755a` | Faded caption ink, secondary text |
| `--accent` | `#c9402e` | Postcard red — the stamp, one per page |
| `--line` | `#3d2f221f` | Divider hairlines (alpha allowed on lines) |

## Type

- **Display:** Bungee (souvenir lettering, big and cheerful — "GREETINGS FROM", 3–6rem)
- **Body:** Karla (1rem, the note on the back of the card)
- **Mono:** Courier Prime (postmarks — "MAILED FROM DOVER · JUL 14")

Google Fonts: `Bungee` `Karla:wght@400;500;700` `Courier+Prime:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Rack dividers: sections separated by labeled tabs, like the rack's row cards.
- Postcard aspect ratios (3:2) for imagery; cards sit at slight rotations.
- Captions under everything, set small and faded like printed captions.
- Radius: `4px`. Rounded card corners.

## Motion

Cards straighten on hover (transform only) — the little wobble of pulling a
card off the rack. Nothing else moves.
With reduced motion: cards stay straight.

## Do

- Write the back of the card like a human: "Weather is perfect. Wish you were here."
- Caption everything in faded small type — postcards always explain themselves.
- Spend the postcard red once per viewport: the stamp or the CTA.
- Microcopy from the rack: "Six for five dollars. The lighthouse ones go first."

## Don't

- No purple or blue gradients — the card stock is flat and sun-faded, never a gradient.
- No travel kitsch beyond the format itself: no plane-takeoff clip art, no passport stamps as icons.
- No emoji icons; no lorem — the cards have real captions.
- No full-bleed stock hero with a dark overlay. Postcards are small and honest.

## The one weird thing

A postmark stamp, slightly rotated, stamped over the hero — "SENT WITH LOVE" —
in mono, with a wobbly hand-set date. Every page gets its own date, like it
was actually mailed from somewhere.
