## Type

- **Display:** Special Elite (stamped shop signage — "FIXED WHILE YOU WAIT", 3–5rem)
- **Body:** Courier Prime (1rem, the typed work order — every letter earns its place)
- **Mono:** IBM Plex Mono (serial numbers, ticket stubs — "SN 4-28117", "JOB 114")

Google Fonts: `Special+Elite` `Courier+Prime:wght@400;700` `IBM+Plex+Mono:wght@400;500`

## Spacing & shape

- Services are stamped repair tickets: a round shop stamp overlapping one corner.
- Parts-tray grids hold the details — small compartments, each with one fact.
- The bench is a wide, quiet surface: generous margins, nothing cramped.
- Radius: `2px`. Job cards, barely eased.

## Motion

The headline arrives like typebars tapping — letters in with a small
translateY and opacity, staggered 40ms. Ticket cards settle with a fade-and-rise
(transform and opacity only). With reduced motion: the page arrives already typed.

## Do

- Write repair-ticket copy: "Machine in Tuesday, out Friday. Sticky spacebar, fixed."
- Use serial numbers and job numbers in the microcopy: "SN 4-28117 — ready for pickup."
- Post honest turnaround times; a repair shop's word is its warranty.
- Keep the shop stamp round, red, and slightly rotated — stamped, not printed.

## Don't

- No purple or blue gradients — the bench is paper and steel, never a glow.
- No terminal-green-on-black; that's the digital thing this isn't.
- No stock typewriter photo with a dark overlay; the work order is the image.
- No emoji, no lorem — the ticket is always filled out.

## The one weird thing

Every service is a stamped repair ticket — "IN: sticky spacebar / OUT: types
like new" — with a round shop stamp overlapping the corner in ribbon red.
