# Memphis Milano

> 80s postmodern play: squiggles, terrazzo, pastel-neon clashes.
> Deliberate kitsch that refuses to apologize.

| Token | Value | Role |

## Type

- **Display:** Shrikhand (rounded, chunky, irreverent — headlines are the party)
- **Body:** Space Grotesk (400/500; straight talk that keeps the chaos readable)
- **Mono:** Space Mono (labels, captions, ticket stubs)

Google Fonts: `Shrikhand` `Space+Grotesk:wght@400;500;700`
`Space+Mono:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Overlap is allowed: squiggles, dots, and terrazzo tiles sit ON the layout, not in it.
- Tilt things slightly: cards at −2°/2°, stickers rotated, badges askew.
- Radius: mixed — circles everywhere (dots, badges), squared cards, squiggle edges.
- Rhythm: bouncy. Big gaps, then a cluster of confetti marks. Never evenly spaced.

## Motion

Motion should wobble, pop, and spring. Elements enter with a slight overshoot,
squiggles draw themselves, stickers jiggle on hover like they're pinned on.
Color blocks can pulse gently. Speed is quick but not frantic — this is a
party, not an emergency. Rotation on hover is encouraged.

## Do

- Scatter terrazzo chips and squiggles around section edges and behind type — confetti framing.
- Let Shrikhand headlines tilt, overlap shapes, and touch the margins.
- Clash pastel and neon on purpose: mint with hot pink, lavender with teal.
- Write copy like a party flyer: "BRING FRIENDS.", "Snacks at 7.", exclamation marks earned, not feared.

## Don't

- No beige seriousness, no corporate grids, no "premium" minimalism.
- No more than two squiggle types per section — chaos needs a budget.
- No thin delicate type anywhere; everything is chunky or outlined.
- No gradients except on a retro sun, and even then, sparingly.

## The one weird thing

Add one tactile sticker per page: a "Certified Fun" badge, a starburst price
tag, or a peeled-corner sticker element. Memphis was about cheap printed
ephemera — stickers, badges, flyers. One physical-feeling decal grounds the
digital chaos. Pick one.

Toy shops, party planners, creative studios.
