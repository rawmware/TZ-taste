# Sahara Ochre

> The craft market at golden hour: terracotta, clay, and spice tones, handmade texture everywhere, the smell of kiln smoke baked into the page. Earth as warmth, never as minimalism.

## Type

- **Display:** DM Serif Display — a warm, slightly plump serif that feels hand-lettered on a shop sign. Use it for shop names, product titles, and market-day headlines, with italic accents on key words.
- **Body:** Karla — friendly and readable for product descriptions, origin stories, and maker bios. It stays warm at small sizes where DM Serif Display would go soft.
- **Mono:** Space Mono — price tags, stall numbers, weight and origin codes ("STAL 07 — 500G"). Tags should look printed on little paper labels.

Google Fonts: `DM+Serif+Display:ital@0;1`, `Karla:wght@400;600;700`, `Space+Mono:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Organic and unhurried: generous padding, slightly uneven rhythm — rows can vary in height like market stalls.
- Shapes are hand-drawn soft: large radii, occasional arch-topped frames (like a doorway or a kiln opening).
- Dividers are wavy or double-ruled in sand tones, never straight black lines.
- Price tags and labels tilt a degree or two, tied on like string tags.

## Motion

Gentle and tactile: cards lift slightly with a soft shadow on hover, like picking up a pot to look at it. Fades are warm and slow. Nothing should move fast — handmade things take time, and the motion should say so.

## Do

- Texture the background: a subtle grain or weave pattern in the sand tone makes the whole page feel touchable.
- Give every product an origin line in mono: where it was made, by whom, in what kiln — provenance is the design.
- Use arch shapes for images and feature frames; the arch is this DNA's signature.
- Layer terracotta over spice over clay in bands of color, like stacked bowls.

## Don't

- Don't go modernist-architecture minimal — this is craft warmth, not gallery concrete; keep the texture and the clutter of detail.
- Don't use cold grays or blues — the palette is earth-bound and that's non-negotiable.
- Don't set product names in the body font — DM Serif Display is the shop sign; it should always be visible on a product card.
- Don't fake imperfection with heavy grunge filters — restraint keeps it artisanal instead of dusty.

## The one weird thing

Make the price tag a real object: a little rotated rectangle in the clay surface color with a punched hole and a short string (CSS pseudo-element) tying it to the corner of each product card, mono price stamped inside. Or add a "kiln stamp" — a circular terracotta badge with the maker's initials in serif, pressed onto one corner of the hero. Pick one.
