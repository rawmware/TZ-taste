# Sewing Pattern

> Tissue thin as breath, dashed lines that mean cut here, notches that have to match.
> Measure twice, sew once.

## Type

- **Display:** DM Serif Display (pattern-envelope titles — "THE EVERYDAY JACKET", 3–5rem)
- **Body:** Nunito Sans (1rem, the instruction voice — patient, step by step)
- **Mono:** Space Mono (measurements, piece marks — "CUT 2", "58 IN WIDE")

Google Fonts: `DM+Serif+Display` `Nunito+Sans:opsz,wght@6..12,400;6..12,600;6..12,700` `Space+Mono:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Cards are edged with dashed "cut here" borders — the line is the design.
- Grainline arrows run through sections as directional motifs, always straight.
- Size charts are real tables: bust, waist, hip, in inches, no gaps.
- Radius: `0px`. Tissue doesn't round.

## Motion

Pattern pieces lift and straighten on hover (rotate from -1deg to 0, transform
only). Notches pulse once on load (opacity). With reduced motion: the pieces
stay pinned to the table.

## Do

- Write instruction-style copy: "Pin, then cut. Don't stretch the bias."
- Label everything like a real sheet: "PIECE 3 — FRONT, CUT 2", "CUT 1 ON FOLD".
- Set size charts as complete, honest tables — a sewer will check your math.
- Keep one berry accent per viewport: the notches or the CTA, never both.

## Don't

- No purple or blue gradients — tissue is flat paper, chalk is flat chalk.
- No cutesy pin-cushion clip art; the tools are drawn or absent.
- No lorem in the size chart — someone will actually sew from it.
- No emoji — the instruction voice doesn't need them.

## The one weird thing

Every section is a numbered pattern piece — "PIECE 3 — FRONT, CUT 2" — with a
grainline arrow and matching notches, edged in a dashed cut line.
