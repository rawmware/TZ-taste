# Corner Deli

> Daytime awning: the slicer is already warm, the ticket rail is full by eleven,
> and everybody's order is remembered whether they want it to be or not.

| Token | Value | Role |

## Type

- **Display:** Bebas Neue (awning signage, tall and condensed — "HOT PASTRAMI", 3–6rem)
- **Body:** Karla (1rem, counter-talk plain talk)
- **Mono:** Special Elite (deli tickets — "NOW SERVING #47")

Google Fonts: `Bebas+Neue` `Karla:wght@400;500;700` `Special+Elite`

## Spacing & shape

- Awning stripes mark section breaks — red and cream bands, flat, honest.
- Menu boards in the darker paper tone, dense with real items and prices.
- Ticket-rail density for lists: number, item, price, done.
- Radius: `2px`. Paper and chalk, not plastic.

## Motion

The "NOW SERVING" number flips once on load (transform only), like the rail
being updated. Everything else is as still as the chalkboard.
With reduced motion: the number is already set.

## Do

- Write the menu board like a human: "The #4. You know the #4."
- Price everything — deli menus never hide the price.
- Spend the tomato red once per viewport: the awning stripe or the CTA.
- Microcopy from the counter: "Extra pickles? You got it."

## Don't

- No purple or blue gradients — the deli is flat paper and daylight, never a gradient.
- No food kitsch: no cartoon sandwiches with faces, no chef-hat clip art.
- No emoji icons; no lorem — the menu board has real sandwiches.
- No dark, moody food photography. This is 11 a.m. sunlight through the awning.

## The one weird thing

A "NOW SERVING" ticket number pinned in the header — "NOW SERVING #47" —
in typewriter mono. It ticks up one number per visit, like you're actually
standing at the counter waiting your turn.

## Best for

Delis, cafes, sandwich shops, neighborhood services.
