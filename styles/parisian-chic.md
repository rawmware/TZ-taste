# Parisian Chic

> Haute couture on paper: black, white, and cream with a high-contrast Didot-style serif, generous air, and the kind of restraint that makes one red lip the whole outfit.

## Tokens

| Token | Value | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--bg` | `#faf7f2` | cream silk |
| `--surface` | `#ffffff` | crisp white card |
| `--ink` | `#141210` | noir ink |
| `--muted` | `#8a8178` | stone gray |
| `--accent` | `#141210` | noir (used with weight, not color) |
| `--line` | `#e4ddd2` | champagne hairline |
| `--accent-2` | `#a63d40` | rouge lipstick — used sparingly, one element per page |

## Type

- **Display:** Playfair Display — the Didone voice: high contrast, thin hairlines, dramatic swashes at large sizes. Headlines get tight letter-spacing and sentence case, like a maison's nameplate.
- **Body:** Jost — a geometric humanist for descriptions, notes, and captions. Light weights with generous line-height; it should feel like good paper.
- **Mono:** Space Mono — used rarely and deliberately: collection numbers ("Nº 5"), dates, sizes, price codes. Small, letterspaced, in muted.

Google Fonts: `Playfair+Display:ital,wght@0,400;0,600;1,400`, `Jost:wght@300;400;500`, `Space+Mono:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Air is the luxury: sections breathe with enormous vertical padding; density is the enemy of chic.
- Hairline rules (1px) in `--line` separate everything — ateliers divide with lines, not boxes.
- Corners are square or nearly so; couture doesn't do bubbles.
- One column, centered, narrow measure (60–65ch) for editorial passages.

## Motion

Barely there: slow crossfades, a gentle reveal as sections enter. Hover states are a single underline drawn left-to-right under links, thin as a seam. Nothing bounces, nothing pops — movement should feel like a model walking, not dancing.

## Do

- Let typography be the design — one great serif headline on cream with a hairline rule does more than any layout trick.
- Use the rouge accent once, decisively: a single lipstick-red detail (one word italicized, one thin rule, one CTA).
- Number everything like a collection: "Collection Nº 2", "Atelier — since 2019".
- Keep images large, bordered by white, captioned in small italic serif — like plates in a lookbook.

## Don't

- Don't reach for gradients or color variety — noir, cream, one red. That's the whole wardrobe.
- Don't use this DNA for anything editorial-literary — the fashion register is the point; keep it about craft, fit, and material.
- Don't set display type small — Playfair's contrast needs size to work; at small sizes it reads as a generic serif.
- Don't crowd the grid — if you're adding a third column, you're designing for someone else.

## The one weird thing

Give the site a "nameplate": a fixed, hairline-bordered mono strip at the very top — `MAISON — Nº 5 — PARIS` — that never scrolls away, like a label sewn into the garment. Or style the footer as a care label: tiny mono text, centered, with washing-instruction humor ("Handle with care. Do not tumble dry."). Pick one.

## Best for

Fashion houses, perfume brands, bridal ateliers, jewelers, interior stylists.

## Lineage

The typographic restraint of couture house nameplates and invitation cards; the atelier habit of naming and numbering everything; the principle that in luxury, the empty space is the most expensive material.
