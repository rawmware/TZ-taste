# Midnight Railway

> The night train platform: deep navy, brass fittings, timetables set with total confidence.

## Type

- **Display:** DM Serif Display, large, unhurried — set like a destination board: "LONDON — EDINBURGH"
- **Body:** DM Sans 400/500, timetable-adjacent clarity
- **Mono:** Space Mono for departure times, platform numbers, prices

Google Fonts: `DM+Serif+Display:ital@0;1` `DM+Sans:wght@400;500;700` `Space+Mono:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Timetable grids: columns of times, destinations, statuses — the layout is the schedule.
- Ticket-stub elements: perforated edges, stamped badges, compartment numbers.
- Radius: `2px`. Riveted, not rounded.
- Brass rules separate sections; oxblood panels carry emphasis.

## Motion

Departure-board motion: flip-updates on key numbers, slow horizontal pans on
route imagery, stamped-in reveals. Everything moves like it's on rails —
constrained, linear, on time.

## Do

- Timetables as real UI: departures, durations, classes of service
- Destination-board headlines: "PLATFORM 9 — NOW BOARDING"
- Brass accents used like fittings: sparingly, always functional
- Window views: dark, cinematic imagery cropped like a train window

## Don't

- No day-travel brightness — this DNA is night only
- No playful rounded illustration styles; the mood is 1930s confidence, not cartoons
- No clutter: a platform sign says one thing clearly
- No neon — brass is the metal here

## The one weird thing

Every site needs one board. Either the hero is a live departure board (times,
destinations, statuses — real content) or each section opens with a stamped
ticket header ("ADMIT ONE — SECTION 03"). One timetable idiom, committed. Pick one.

## Best for

Travel, hospitality, whisky brands, boutique hotels, rail journeys, luggage.
