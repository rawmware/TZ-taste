# Tropical Brutalism

> Brazilian concrete breathing: raw gray mass, lush green, hard sunlight and deep shadow.

## Type

- **Display:** Anton, uppercase, huge — set like a building name cast in concrete
- **Body:** Space Grotesk 400/500, earthy and readable
- **Mono:** Space Mono for specs: materials, coordinates, sun angles

Google Fonts: `Anton` `Space+Grotesk:wght@400;500;700` `Space+Mono:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- Massive concrete blocks of layout: heavy rectangles, deep shadows (`box-shadow: 16px 16px 0 rgba(32,36,30,.25)`), stacked like slabs.
- Light is a design tool: split sections into sun-half and shadow-half.
- Radius: `0` or architectural `2px`. Nothing organic in the geometry — the green provides the organic.
- Breathe: brutalist buildings breathe through courtyards; pages breathe through huge white-of-plaster gaps.

## Motion

Slow like afternoon sun. Parallax shadow shifts, sections that reveal with a
long ease, hover states that deepen shadows rather than bounce. Think of it as
thermal mass: slow to warm, slow to cool.

## Do

- Big architectural photography, treated warm — or pure abstract geometry if no photos
- Concrete texture (subtle noise), never clean vectors
- Sun/ochre as the "light" accent: use it wherever sunlight would fall
- Spec-plate details: coordinates, materials, climate notes as mono captions

## Don't

- No gray-on-gray corporate brutalism — the green is mandatory, it's the whole point
- No cold blue steel-and-glass language; concrete and plants, not curtain walls
- No tiny delicate type against the massive blocks — type must hold the wall
- No glassmorphism, no blur — solid mass only

## The one weird thing

Choose your sun. Either the whole site has one fixed light direction (shadows
always fall the same way, ochre always on the same side) or the light "moves"
as you scroll, with ochre blocks shifting down the page. One sun, one rule. Pick one.

## Best for

Architecture firms, hotels, botanical brands, landscape studios, eco-retreats.

## Lineage

Mid-century Brazilian modernism: honest concrete, deep shade, buildings that
garden themselves — architecture as climate, not object.
