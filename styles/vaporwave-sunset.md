# Vaporwave Sunset

> Retro-futurist sunset grids in peach, coral, and teal.
> A chrome sun that never quite sets.

## Tokens

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `#1a1216` | Warm near-black, dusk sky |
| `--surface` | `#241a20` | Panels, cards, cassette-window frames |
| `--ink` | `#f7e8d8` | Warm cream text |
| `--muted` | `#9a7f72` | Dusty rose-gray, captions |

## Type

- **Display:** Monoton (chrome-letter signage — used ONLY for the hero headline and section titles)
- **Body:** Space Grotesk (400/500, relaxed — keeps the nostalgia readable)
- **Mono:** Space Mono (track listings, dates, catalog numbers — uppercase)

Google Fonts: `Monoton` `Space+Grotesk:wght@400;500;700`
`Space+Mono:wght@400;700`

## Spacing & shape

- The horizon line is sacred: a grid that recedes, always below the fold's first breath.
- One chrome sun per hero — striped, low, centered or off-center but never duplicated.
- Radius: mixed — retro rounded rectangles on cards (`8px`), sharp tech frames on panels.
- Airy by default; density comes from pattern (grids, palm silhouettes, scanlines), not content.

## Motion

Slow drift. The sun pulses gently, the grid scrolls toward you at walking pace,
chrome type shimmers once on load. Everything should feel like late afternoon
with no deadline. Scanlines sweep rarely. Never frantic — the sun is setting,
not exploding.

## Do

- Keep the palette warm: peach, coral, teal, cream — sunset colors, and the rule is NEVER purple-blue.
- Use the perspective grid as your primary background texture; it's the DNA's signature.
- Write copy like a cassette liner: evocative, short, slightly wistful. "Side A: arrivals."
- Let Monoton be the only chrome on the page — everything else stays flat and warm.

## Don't

- No purple-blue gradient washes — the single most common vaporwave slop signal.
- No Monoton for body copy or UI labels; chrome letters are for display only.
- No stock "aesthetic" filler (statues, Greek busts) — build atmosphere with grids, sun, and type.
- No icy or sterile tone; this is a warm memory of a future that never happened.

## The one weird thing

Add one analog detail per page: a cassette-style track listing with runtimes,
a "CH-03" channel label in the corner, or a VHS-style date stamp. The future
here is remembered through 1986 hardware — one hardware artifact keeps it
honest. Pick one.
