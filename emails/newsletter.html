Five books worth your October, and one worth skipping.

Good morning. Prize season is over, the shortlists are settled, and the bookshop tables are lying to you. Here is what actually held up.

1 · THE RETELLING THAT WON EVERYTHING

Percival Everett's James is the year's best book, full stop.

The Huckleberry Finn story told by Jim, in Jim's voice, and it reframes every page of Twain you half-remember from school. Won the National Book Award and the Pulitzer. If you read one literary novel this fall, this is the one.

Doubleday · 320 pp · $28.00 hardcover  ·  Read our full review

2 · THE QUIET BLOCKBUSTER

Sally Rooney's Intermezzo is grief with a chess clock.

Two brothers in Dublin, mourning their father, fumbling two very different love affairs. Rooney's dialogue is doing more with less than ever — the chess scenes alone are worth the cover price. Her most mature novel by a mile.

Farrar, Straus and Giroux · 448 pp · $28.00 hardcover  ·  Read our full review

3 · THE ONE YOU CAN FINISH TONIGHT

Samantha Harvey's Orbital is 136 pages of pure awe.

Six astronauts, one day, sixteen sunrises over a warming Earth. The 2024 Booker winner is the rare prize novel that reads like a poem and lands like a warning. Keep it on the nightstand; you'll reread the typhoon passage.

Grove Atlantic · 136 pp · $26.00 hardcover  ·  Read our full review
