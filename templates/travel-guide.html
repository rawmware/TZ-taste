The unglamorous pages

Practical, honestly

The stuff that decides whether a trip sings or stumbles.

Money

Dirhams are king. Cards work in cities; cash rules everywhere else. ATMs are plentiful — withdraw 2,000 MAD at a time to dodge fees. Haggling: start at 40%, meet near 65%, smile throughout.

Getting around

Rent the small car. A Dacia Logan handles mountain passes and medina-edge parking. Trains link the big cities beautifully; buses (CTM) cover the rest. Never drive the desert roads after dark.

When to go

October to April for the south and desert; May and September for the coast. August in Marrakech is a dare, not a plan. Ramadan is magical and slower — book dinners ahead.

Staying well

Drink bottled water. Eat where the grill smoke is thickest and the turnover fastest. Pharmacies are excellent and pharmacists diagnose generously — for anything serious, clinics in the big cities are solid.

Pack light. Pack right.

The tested packing list from three editions. Everything here survived at least two trips; everything cut didn't survive one.

  • A scarf — sun, dust, mosque visits, improvised picnic blanket
  • Broken-in walking shoes with real grip (medina cobbles are treacherous)
  • A headlamp — for the dunes, the power cuts, and the 5 a.m. gorge
  • Layers, not bulk: desert nights drop 20 degrees
  • A small daypack that zips shut (souk crowds are skilled)
  • Cash in small denominations — the 200-dirham note breaks hearts
  • An unlocked phone + local SIM (20 MAD at any tabac)
  • Patience, in industrial quantities
