CHAPTER ONE — ARRIVAL

already hot. 静

Stillwater is a tea room, not a cafe. Eight teas. One quiet room. No music, no laptops, no rush. You stay for one pot or three hours — the room does not check the clock, so neither do we.

CHAPTER TWO — THE FLIGHT

Tea, in the order
it should be drunk

We serve tea as a flight: four cups, light to dark, each one steeped to its own temperature. Order the full flight, or stop anywhere along the way. Refills of hot water are free and unlimited.

FIRST CUP

Spring Sencha

煎茶 · shincha, first harvest

Steamed green tea from Uji, picked in late April. Bright and grassy, the way a garden smells after rain. The cup we give people who say they don't like green tea.

70°C · 90 seconds · two steeps

SECOND CUP

Shade-Grown Gyokuro

玉露 · the slow one

Three weeks under shade cloth before picking. Thick, savory, almost brothy — drink it in small sips, the way you would a good soup.

55°C · 120 seconds · three steeps

THIRD CUP

Roasted Hojicha

焙じ茶 · evening tea

Green tea roasted over charcoal until it turns the color of toast. Low in caffeine, warm as a blanket. The cup most regulars end the day on.

90°C · 30 seconds · three steeps

Ceremonial grade, whisked in the bowl in front of you. Bitter at first sip, sweet at the third. We serve it with one piece of seasonal wagashi.

The full flight — all four cups, in order — is $24. It takes about ninety minutes. Most people finish the fourth cup and order the first one again. We are ready for this.

CHAPTER THREE — THE ROOM

Twelve seats.
One long table.
No music.

What the room asks of you

Speak quietly or not at all. Phones stay in pockets — there is a basket by the door, and yes, people use it. Children are welcome when they can be quiet; we have cushions, not high chairs.

What the room gives back

Light from the north windows, which never glares. Water that is always hot. A keeper who notices when your pot is empty and refills it without a word.

  • RULE 01No laptops. The table is for tea and the people across from you.
  • RULE 02No photographs of other guests. Your own cup is fair game.
  • RULE 03Stay as long as you like. We have never once asked anyone to leave.
CHAPTER FOUR — THE KEEPER
"I opened Stillwater because I missed the version of myself that sits still. The tea is just the excuse."
— HANA MORI, KEEPER OF THE ROOM

Hana trained in Kyoto for six years and spent four more running a kissaten in Osaka before moving here. She chooses the eight teas, fires the kiln-warmed cups, and decides when the room is full. Trust her judgment; it is the whole business model.
