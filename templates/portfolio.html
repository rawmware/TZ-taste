Good identities are not decorated; they are decided. For seven years I've worked with founders and directors who know their business is better than it looks, and we fix that — slowly, on paper first, in rooms together. I take four commissions a year. Each one gets my full attention and my full stubbornness.

My studio is small on purpose: me, a printmaker I share a press with, and a developer who tolerates my opinions about letterspacing. We have never missed a deadline we set ourselves, which is why I set so few.

How a commission goes

PHASE I

The long conversation

Two weeks of talking, visiting, and looking at everything you've ever made. I take notes on paper.

PHASE II

The shortlist

Three directions, presented in the room, pinned to the wall. We argue. One survives.

PHASE III

The system

The surviving direction becomes rules: type, color, layout, voice. Rules you can actually follow.

PHASE IV

The handover

A printed manual, digital files, and one training session with whoever touches the brand daily.

What a commission covers

  • Identity — the full instrument

    Naming, logo, typography, color, and a printed manual your team can actually follow. Includes one training session.
