In praise of slow reading

By June Okonkwo · 6 min left

The brass hairline above fills as you read — with chapter marks for the essay's four movements, and an honest estimate of what's left. No popups, no "you're almost done" nudges. Just a quiet gauge.

Reading used to have a physical progress bar: the thickness of pages in your right hand versus your left. Screens took that away and replaced it with nothing, which is why long articles feel longer than they are. A visible, calm indicator restores the contract: here's where you are, here's what's left, no surprises.

The chapter marks matter more than the fill. Knowing the essay shifts from argument to evidence at the one-third mark lets a reader decide to continue with intent rather than momentum. Skimming becomes a choice instead of a drift.

Time-remaining estimates should be honest and quiet. "6 min left" updates as you read; it never shouts. The moment a progress indicator becomes gamified — streaks, badges, confetti — it stops serving the reader and starts serving the metric.

Build it with a hairline, a fill, and restraint. The best reading progress indicator is the one you notice exactly twice: once at the start, once when you realize you're nearly done and glad you stayed.
